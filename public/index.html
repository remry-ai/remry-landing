You carry a lot in your head.

Who reports to whom, which team owns what, where each goal stands, why you chose that vendor, what you promised after the last 1:1. Your team’s tools don’t hold your version of it.

How it works

  1. You talk, Claude files.

    Say “Dana moved from Platform to Payments” and Claude makes the change. No forms.

  2. It doesn’t forget.

    It’s stored as you said it, not summarised into a memory that fades. Claude looks it up instead of recalling it.

  3. It keeps the shape of things.

    People have managers, projects have owners, goals have dated check-ins. So “what depends on the payments API?” gets the same answer every time.

  4. Nothing leaves your computer.

    Your notebooks live in a folder on your Mac or PC. You read them in an app beside Claude.

What using it looks like

An org change

You: Dana Park joined as a senior engineer, on Platform, reporting to Alice.

Claude adds Dana, sets her lead and team. For a reorg, edit the org map by hand.

After a 1:1

You: Note that Dana wants to lead the migration — she’d change the on-call rotation first. Remind me to book a 1:1.

The note lands on Dana’s page, dated, and the reminder becomes a todo. Months later, “what do I know about Dana?” brings it all back.

Goals with real numbers

You: Platform’s H2 goal is p95 latency under 200ms, from 480. We’re at 310 now.

Each check-in adds to the history instead of overwriting it, and a goal that trails its pace is flagged “Behind pace”.

Writing up the quarter

You: Write up Platform’s quarter as a doc — delivery and reliability, with charts.

Claude builds it from what’s already in the notebook, charts included. Export to PDF with Pro.

How is this different from…

…your assistant’s own memory?
It decides what to keep and summarises it. Remry keeps what you said, where you can see it.
…a memory server, like Basic Memory?
Those store free text. Remry knows what a person, project and goal are, so it can answer “which goals are behind pace?” instead of searching for the word “goal”.
…Obsidian, Notion or Apple Notes with an assistant connected?
There you organise the pages. Here you say what happened, and it’s filed in the right place.
…Linear, Notion or Lattice?
Those hold the team’s shared version. Remry holds yours, privately, and links back to them.

Private by design

  • Local only

    Files on your computer, with hourly backups. No cloud sync.

  • No accounts

    Nothing to sign up for or log in to.

  • The app never calls a model

    Claude works on it from outside. The app’s only request is checking GitHub for updates.

  • Offline license key

    Checked on your computer. No license server.

  • 30-day refunds

    Not for you? Ask within 30 days.

Questions

Which assistants does it work with?

Claude, today: Claude desktop (Chat and Cowork) and Claude Code. Run Cowork sessions on your computer, not in the cloud, so Claude can reach your notebook.

Which platforms does it run on?

Apple silicon Macs and Windows (x64). The Windows installer isn’t signed yet, so Windows warns the first time: choose More info → Run anyway.

What happens if my Pro license lapses?

Search, branding and PDF export lock. Nothing else changes and no data is lost.

Where is my data?

~/Library/Application Support/Remry/ on a Mac, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Remry\ on Windows. It stays there if you uninstall.

Who makes it?

One person, jweatherby.dev, building in the open. Expect it to change.
