You carry a lot in your head.

Who reports to whom. Which team owns which project. Where a goal stands this month. Why you chose that vendor. What you promised to follow up on after a 1:1.

Your team's tools hold the team's version of things. Yours is spread across your memory, a few docs and whatever you wrote down last time.

How it works

  1. You talk, Claude files.

    Say “Dana moved from Platform to Payments” and Claude makes the change. There is no form to fill in, and the app doesn’t need to be open.

  2. It doesn’t forget.

    What you tell it is stored as you said it, not summarised into a memory that fades. Claude looks it up instead of trying to recall it.

  3. It keeps the shape of things.

    A person has a manager, a project has an owner, a goal has dated check-ins. So “what depends on the payments API?” gets the same answer every time, not a search result.

  4. Nothing leaves your computer.

    No accounts, no keys, no sync. Your notebooks live in a folder on your Mac or PC, and you read them in an app beside Claude.

What using it looks like

An org change

You: Dana Park joined as a senior engineer, on Platform, reporting to Alice.

Claude adds Dana, sets the reporting line and puts her on the team. Redrawing a reorganisation? Edit the org map by hand.

After a 1:1

You: Note that Dana wants to lead the migration — she’d change the on-call rotation first. Remind me to book a 1:1.

The note goes on Dana’s page in your words, with the date, and the reminder becomes a todo. Months later, “what do I know about Dana?” brings back her notes, todos, docs and goals.

Goals with real numbers

You: Platform’s H2 goal is p95 latency under 200ms, from 480. We’re at 310 now.

Each check-in adds to the goal’s history instead of overwriting a number. The chart shows the pace you need, and a goal that trails it is flagged “Behind pace”.

Writing up the quarter

You: Write up Platform’s quarter as a doc — delivery and reliability, with charts.

Claude builds it from what’s already in the notebook. Docs are markdown with charts drawn from figures you give Claude, and export to PDF with Remry Pro.

How is this different from…

…your assistant’s own memory?
It decides what to keep and summarises it. Remry keeps exactly what you said, where you can see it.
…a memory server, like Basic Memory?
Those store free text and links. Remry knows what a person, project and goal are, and turns away input that doesn’t fit. That’s why it can answer “which goals are behind pace?” instead of searching for the word “goal”. It also has an app to read it in.
…Obsidian, Notion or Apple Notes with an assistant connected?
There you organise the pages. Here you say what happened, and it’s filed in the right place.
…Linear, Notion or Lattice?
Those hold the team’s shared version. Remry holds yours, privately, and links back to them. Ask Claude to bring a project in and it copies what matters to you and updates the same entry next time.

Private by design

Notes about your team are sensitive. Remry is built so they never leave your computer.

  • Local only

    Your notebooks are files in a folder on your computer. No cloud sync. Remry copies each notebook every hour when something changed, on the same disk.

  • No accounts

    No sign-up, no keys, nothing to log in to. Work and home can be separate notebooks, and nothing links across them.

  • The app never calls a model

    Claude works on your notebook from outside. The only request the app makes is the desktop app asking GitHub for updates, and it sends nothing about you or your notebooks.

  • Offline license key

    A Pro key is checked on your computer. Remry never contacts a license server.

  • 30-day refunds

    Not for you? Ask within 30 days for a refund.

Questions

Which assistants does it work with?

Claude, today: Claude desktop (Chat and Cowork) and Claude Code. The Remry plugin brings the tools Claude uses and a skill that teaches it how the notebook works. Run Cowork sessions on your computer, not in the cloud, so Claude can reach your notebook.

Which platforms does it run on?

Macs with Apple silicon, and Windows (x64). The Mac app is signed and notarized by Apple, so it opens like any other app. The Windows installer isn’t code-signed yet, so the first time you run it Windows warns you: choose More info → Run anyway.

Do I need to be a developer?

No. Download the app, open it once, and connect Claude from Cowork’s Customize panel or the app’s Claude menu. After that you talk to Claude as usual.

What happens if my Pro license lapses?

Full-text search, branding and PDF export lock. Nothing else changes and no data is lost; a renewed key brings it all back.

Where is my data?

In a folder on your computer: ~/Library/Application Support/Remry/ on a Mac, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Remry\ on Windows. Your notebooks stay there when the app updates, and if you uninstall it. Hourly copies stay on the same disk; on a Mac, Time Machine covers them against losing the disk.

Who makes it?

Remry is one person’s tool, built in the open by jweatherby.dev. Expect it to change.
